2021 survey

Public Safety Committee · August 18, 2021 · Staff report · PDF p. 3

The survey ran from March 4 through May 31, 2021. The City received 2,268 responses overall, but 1,038 ranked the mockups.

Most preferred mockup, among 1,038 rankings
DesignVotesShare reported
Option A – Mesh (Curved-Straight)21620.8%
Option B – Mesh (Curved-Curved)46244.5%

Total rankings1,038100.0%

The report separately records 622 comments, of which 44% preferred that barriers not be installed and that the bridge return to its original state without the temporary chain-link fence. Its Option C footnote says 59% listed that option as their least favorite. The footnote does not give a separate question count.

The percentage in the total row is calculated from the three percentages shown. People chose whether to take part, so their answers do not necessarily represent Pasadena residents as a whole. Design rankings and written comments were counted separately, using different totals. They should not be treated as the same result or compared directly with the later survey to show a change in opinion.

2021 and 2022 fiscal sections

Public Safety Committee · August 18, 2021 · Fiscal section · PDF p. 5

The fiscal section reports $1.05 million appropriated and spent on installation and maintenance of temporary fencing, mockup design and installation, environmental assessment, conceptual design, and outreach. It estimates $4–6 million to complete design and construction. This paragraph is a hand-checked summary, not a verbatim transcription.

Public Safety Committee · September 21, 2022 · Fiscal section · PDF p. 4

The 2022 section again describes the $1.05 million spent on those earlier activities and records an additional $2 million in ARPA funding in FY2022 for future phases. It says the actual cost cannot be determined until an option has been selected and its design completed. This is a hand-checked summary.

These reports do not account for every budget approval, transfer, or payment through 2026. Adding their figures together will not show the money currently available. They do not establish whether the federal ARPA funding met its commitment requirements or other rules.

How the approved project budget changed

The approved project budget reached $3,050,000: $950,000 from the General Fund, $100,000 in Gas Tax funding, and $2 million labeled ARPA, the federal American Rescue Plan Act. In 2025, the Council transferred $130,000 on June 9 and $46,000 on July 21 to the San Rafael Bridge Seismic Retrofit project, reducing the total to $2,874,000. Staff said enough remained to finish design without affecting its progress. Construction still required additional funding.
