Staff advised against use as a substitute

Staffing returned across several meetings

City staff examined cost and practical coverage. They treated human presence as something that could accompany a physical barrier, while repeatedly advising against relying on staffing to replace it.

2017–2018

Patrol was among the alternatives

The task force’s review included police presence, patrols, technology, and physical barriers. It distinguished measures that restrict access from measures that make intervention more likely.

Aug 2021

Around-the-clock staffing was proposed

Hampton proposed staffing the bridge 24 hours a day. City Manager Steve Mermell said it had been suggested before, would likely cost several hundred thousand dollars annually, and was not feasible as a replacement. Gordo developed a Host/Guide-style idea.

Staff explained the coverage problem

Kris Markarian described continuous coverage, multiple personnel, liability concerns, and incidents outside ordinary working hours. Gordo asked for more work on trained security and a daytime human presence. Madison questioned response speed.

Jul 2024

Annual cost estimates were presented

The requested estimates were roughly $600,000 a year for private security and $1.16 million a year for law-enforcement coverage. Justin Jones asked whether staffing was being ruled out because of cost, effectiveness, or both. Staff answered both.

Limits of this finding

These are the City’s coverage estimates and staff assessments, not a controlled trial establishing that every staffing arrangement would fail. The research distinguishes an additional opportunity to intervene from a substitute for restricting access.
