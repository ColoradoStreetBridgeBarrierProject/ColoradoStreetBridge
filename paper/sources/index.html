2. City of Pasadena Public Safety Committee presentation and official minutes, July 19, 2017. The presentation said the bridge began to “earn” its nickname during the Great Depression, reported more than 150 deaths by suicide at the bridge since 1919, including an estimated 79 during the 1930s, showed City fencing and netting drawings from approximately 1932, reported that a police detail at the bridge during the 1930s cost $20,000 a year, and counted 28 deaths during 2006 through 2016. Slide 10 identifies wrought-iron fencing installed during the seismic retrofit in the early 1990s. The same presentation described the existing barrier and railing as nearly eight feet high, but identified all 20 alcoves and the bridge ends as places where people could climb over or get around them. The minutes, p. 3, record Tornek’s support for emergency fencing at the alcoves, his recommendation to continue fencing along the ends and approaches, his request that staff engage local architects and engineers on an effective, cost-effective, and aesthetically pleasing response, and Kennedy’s support for alcove fencing on an emergency basis and his view that it would provide time to find a more permanent solution. The minutes also record Hampton supporting planting trees under the bridge, as several public speakers had suggested, continued fencing along the ends and approaches, surveillance cameras, and citizen police patrols. Madison was absent. Slides 23 and 25 discuss interrupted attempts, the possibility of an acute crisis passing, and the evidence against inevitable displacement to another location. PDF pp. 11–12 report an increase in incidents, including suicides and attempts.

5. City of Pasadena, Colorado Bridge Safety presentation, April 18, 2018, PDF pp. 2–3, 8, 18, 24, and 29–32, and Colorado Bridge Task Force Recommendation agenda report, April 23, 2018, pp. 1–3. Page 3 contains the report’s “cushion” and “no verifying scientific data was found” wording, the approximately 20% planting restriction, and the supplemental-measure qualification. Documents the multidisciplinary participation of City health and public-health leadership, Didi Hirsch Mental Health Services, Police, Fire, design and engineering professionals, and preservation advocates, together with the alternatives matrix distinguishing primary physical means restriction from complementary measures. The City’s July 2017 presentation (pp. 20–21) and April 2018 presentation (p. 38) cite A. Hemmer, P. Meier, and T. Reisch, “Comparing Different Suicide Prevention Measures at Bridges and Buildings: Lessons We Have Learned from a National Survey in Switzerland,” PLOS ONE 12, no. 1, 2017, e0169625. The study’s complete-measure category included both vertical barriers and nets. It did not establish that one type was more effective than the other. Pasadena’s recommendation also reflected its local assessment of preservation and emergency-services concerns.
