An analytical history

The fence everyone can see

By Christopher Clark

Reading edition · October 3, 2026 · About 40 minutes, excluding Sources

The main research review covers material through September 1, 2026. Later checks and additions are dated where they appear.

Open image

Figure 1. The bridge after its 1993 restoration, showing its curve and arches across the Arroyo Seco. BCA Associates.

The Colorado Street Bridge has carried two reputations for generations. It’s one of Pasadena’s great landmarks, a 1913 concrete span over the Arroyo Seco that has become part of the city’s identity. It’s also known as “Suicide Bridge.”2,35

The bridge is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, documented by the Historic American Engineering Record, and designated a Pasadena Cultural Heritage Landmark. Pasadena’s Bridge Party, a tradition dating to 1978, celebrates the bridge and the efforts to preserve it.1,37

Its big-screen history reaches back to Charlie Chaplin’s The Kid (1921), which used the bridge for a scene in which Edna Purviance’s character considers suicide.38 Nearly a century later, it appeared in La La Land (2016).39 The bridge has been a setting for stories, a gathering place, and a landmark people have worked to preserve. But its other history never went away.
