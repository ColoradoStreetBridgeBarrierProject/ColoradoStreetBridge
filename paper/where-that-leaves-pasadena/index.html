Where that leaves Pasadena

Fewer people died at the bridge in the first few years after the fence went up. Those lower counts were recorded while access remained physically restricted. They do not establish what would happen without the fence.3,28 At the November 2023 committee meeting, a Police Department lieutenant reported, in an oral update, deaths at the bridge in 2022 and 2023, showing that the risk had not disappeared.3

Auckland, New Zealand, offers a documented warning. Grafton Bridge had suicide barriers for decades before the city removed them in 1996 amid concerns about their appearance, effects on the historic structure, and interference with rescue operations. Researchers recorded three deaths from the bridge in 1992–1995, before removal, and 15 in 1997–2000, after removal. After improved curved-glass barriers were installed in 2003, researchers recorded no suicides from the bridge during the following four years, describing the sequence as a natural experiment in what happens when a physical deterrent is present, removed, and restored. Low death counts may show that a barrier is working. They do not mean a bridge has become permanently “decoupled” from the risk of suicide.29

A city shouldn’t need another rise in deaths to remember why access was restricted in the first place.

The bridge’s appearance remains at the center of Pasadena’s debate.14,16

The record increasingly suggests that Pasadena’s unresolved question is how to maintain effective protection and public support for it, whether the City keeps the existing fence or replaces it. Keeping the existing fence in place while Pasadena considers its next step is also a choice. The chain-link fence was installed in response to an emergency. It was not the finished product of the design process that followed.4,28

The funding discussion shows how a temporary arrangement could become a lasting one. In 2025, the City transferred $176,000 from the design-phase account to another bridge project, though staff said enough remained to finish design. In April 2026, further reallocation was discussed, and Public Works warned that it would leave Pasadena without matching funds for a shortlisted application. Construction funding remained unresolved. Without a funded path to a permanent barrier, the temporary fence could become the long-term answer by default.28,31,41

If Pasadena chooses to retain it, the public needs a clear explanation of that choice. Otherwise, the emergency fence risks becoming the public’s picture of what a suicide barrier has to look like. Frustration with its appearance could then become pressure to remove or weaken the protection it provides.28,29

That is where the unresolved design debate becomes a concern about safety. The longer the temporary fence remains, the easier it becomes to see the fence as the problem. Its effect on the bridge is visible every day. The deaths it may prevent are not.3,28
